# Protocol: Pre-Draft (3.4)

purpose: pre-scene commitment checklist — every dimension must be confirmed before a single line of the scene is drafted
runs: before Claude drafts any chapter or scene; the orchestrator answers it on the chapter card, and the drafter re-confirms at the top of each drafting session
source_ref: collation:1491-1526 [BANNED 3.4 PRE-DRAFT PROTOCOL]

## How the harness uses this

The orchestrator copies the checklist below onto each chapter card and checks every box with a chapter-specific answer (not a bare tick — e.g. for the physical-tells item, name the tells per character). A chapter card with an unanswered box is not ready to draft. The drafter treats an unchecked or vaguely checked box as a blocking defect in the card, not as permission to improvise.

## The protocol, verbatim

> ## **3.4 PRE-DRAFT PROTOCOL**
>
> Before writing any scene, confirm these commitments:
>
> **Structural:**
>
> * \[ \] I will vary sentence rhythm—no mechanical "X, then Y" patterns
> * \[ \] I will not rely on triple-beat lists or staccato fragments for false emphasis
> * \[ \] I will start with character presence, not atmospheric establishing shots
> * \[ \] I will end on action, decision, or consequence—not stillness or summary
>
> **Language:**
>
> * \[ \] I will use no adverbs on dialogue tags
> * \[ \] I will not use "something" as an emotion placeholder
> * \[ \] I will not describe silence/air/atmosphere as an active force
> * \[ \] I will name specific emotions rather than "raw," "weight," or "need"
>
> **Physical Description:**
>
> * \[ \] Each character has distinct physical tells for tension, desire, and restraint
> * \[ \] I will not default to jaw/throat/hand clichés
> * \[ \] Breathing only matters if it changes scene tempo or power dynamic
>
> **Metaphor:**
>
> * \[ \] No gravitational language for attraction
> * \[ \] No temperature as emotion shorthand
> * \[ \] No dead similes (stone in water, physical blow, hangs in air)
> * \[ \] Every comparison must clarify, not decorate

## Card-answer requirements

- Structural: name the planned opening beat (character in action/decision) and the planned closing beat (the action, decision, or consequence the scene ends on).
- Physical Description: list, per on-page character, their distinct tells for tension, desire, and restraint (pull from the character voice cards; jaw/throat/hand defaults are not answers).
- Metaphor: if the scene plans any figuration, state what each comparison clarifies.
- Language: these four are standing commitments; the card confirms them and the mid-draft flagging protocol (checks/protocols/mid_draft_flagging.md) enforces them in flight.
